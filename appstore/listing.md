## description (1126/4000)

clutter shows your photos and videos one at a time, so you can sort your library in small steps.

swipe right or tap keep to keep a photo. swipe left or tap delete to send it to the deletion inbox. tap an album to add the photo to it and move on. you can create a new album in place. tap undo to go back one step.

choose what to go through:
- all photos, oldest first
- random order
- one month at a time, with a count of what is left in each month

clutter remembers what you already reviewed, so you can stop and come back later. start over resets that. you can also skip photos that are already in an album.

nothing is deleted right away. photos you mark for deletion wait in the deletion inbox. look through them, keep any you want back, then delete the rest. iOS asks you to confirm and moves them to recently deleted, where they stay for 30 days.

videos and live photos play right on the card.

no ads, no purchases, no account, no tracking. clutter has no servers and collects no data. it needs full access to your photo library so it can show your photos and work with your albums.

for iPhone with iOS 26 or later.

## app review notes

clutter helps people go through their photo library one item at a time. no account or sign-in is needed.

how to use it:
1. open the app and allow full access to photos.
2. tap "all photos", "random" or a month.
3. swipe right or tap "keep" to keep a photo. swipe left or tap "delete" to move it to the deletion inbox. tap an album name to add the photo to that album. "new album" creates an album and adds the photo to it. the round arrow button is undo.
4. open the deletion inbox with the tray button in the top bar, or from the home screen when items are waiting. tap a photo to preview it or keep it. tap "delete" at the bottom to delete the rest.

why full photo library access is needed: the app reviews the whole library, counts what is left per month, lists the user's albums and adds photos to them. albums cannot be read or changed with limited access, so the app asks for full access and explains how to turn it on in settings if it is not granted.

deletion: the app never deletes anything on its own. deleting always goes through the system confirmation dialog shown by iOS, and confirmed items go to recently deleted, where they can be recovered for 30 days.

the app makes no network requests of its own and collects no data. the only data it stores is a small local file with the ids of reviewed photos, the deletion inbox and one setting.

## app privacy

data not collected. clutter has no servers, analytics, ads or accounts, and everything it reads or stores stays on the device.

## age rating

4+. answer "none" or "no" to every question. the app has no user-generated content shared with others, no web access, no chat and no purchases. it only shows the user's own photos.
